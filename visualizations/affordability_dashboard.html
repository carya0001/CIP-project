How to read the burden

  • Premium: average monthly premium of an adult in the standard model with CHF 300 deductible, mean over all insurers and then over the premium regions of a canton (each counted equally), × 12.
  • Income: median taxable income of all taxpayers (ESTV). Taxable income is income after deductions and counted per tax unit, so a married couple is one unit.
  • The burden compares one adult's premium with one tax unit's income. It is an indicator for comparing cantons and years, not the real share of a household budget. Premium subsidies are not deducted.
  • Insurance models: the PDF sections "Hausarztmodell" (family doctor), "HMO" and "Weitere Versicherungsmodelle" (other, mostly telemedicine). Each model is averaged over the insurers that offer it, so the set of insurers differs between models. HMO is missing in a few small cantons before 2016.
  • Break-even: a higher deductible pays off only if the yearly premium saving is larger than the extra costs one pays oneself. Between CHF 300 and 2,500 of health costs that is 90% of the costs above CHF 300 (the other 10% are co-payment in both cases), so the break-even is CHF 300 + saving ÷ 0.9.
